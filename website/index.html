How it works

Sorted by your attention, not by app.

New
Everything that arrived since your last check, newest first, shown in full. This is the only part that asks for your attention.
Calls, media and Running
Calls and media players stay at the top. Paused media players can be swiped away when their app allows it. Always-running services, downloads and navigation fold into one “running” pill at the bottom. None of them ever count as new.
Earlier
What you’ve already seen. It stays accessible but recedes: smaller, dimmer, and still in order. A burst from one app folds into a stack. Clear sweeps it all away.
Excluded apps
Switch off an app, or one of its channels, and Chronotif leaves it out. Android’s own notification settings stay exactly as they are.

What counts as a check

  • The panel was open for at least 1.5 seconds, or you interacted with it. When it closes, everything it showed moves to Earlier.
  • On the lock screen, a glance isn’t enough. Notifications move to Earlier when you unlock after they were shown.
  • A notification only comes back to New when it brings something new, like another message in an existing chat. Silent refreshes and progress updates don’t.

Getting there

One gesture away, wherever you are.

  • Edge handle

    A slim pill on the edge of the screen. Tap it, or swipe it down or inward, to open the panel. It takes on your accent colour while something is new. Choose the side and position, or hide it in landscape.

  • Quick Settings tile

    Add the Chronotif tile to Quick Settings. It shows how many notifications are new and opens the panel in one tap.

  • Lock-screen view optional

    Shown over the locked keyguard when the screen turns on: Off, When something is new (the default), or Whenever there are notifications.

Real notifications, not copies.

Chronotif works with the actual notifications, so taps, actions and replies go straight to the app that posted them.

Open
Tap a notification.
Dismiss
Swipe sideways. Swiping a collapsed stack dismisses the whole stack.
Expand
Chevron or long-press, for full text, conversation history, pictures, progress and the app’s actions.
Reply
Expand, tap the reply action, type and send without leaving the panel.

Privacy

Your notifications never leave your phone.

Chronotif reads some of the most private things on your device, so it’s built to keep them there. You don’t have to take our word for it: the source is on GitHub.

  • Notifications never leave

    No accounts, analytics, crash reporters or backend. Your notifications, settings and identifiers are never sent anywhere.

  • Almost nothing stored

    Notifications are mirrored in memory only. The time of your last check, your settings and the names of apps and channels that have posted (for excluding them) are the only things saved, in app-private storage excluded from backups.

  • Lock-screen rules respected

    Follows Android’s lock-screen settings. Secret notifications aren’t shown, private ones are redacted when your settings say so, and opening, actions and replies always go through the system unlock first.

  • Light by design

    No foreground service, wakelocks, alarms or polling. It responds to system events, and has zero third-party dependencies.

  • Updates are opt-in

    The only network code is an optional update check, off by default. Once a day, or when you tap Check for updates, it reads the public release list on GitHub and downloads the new APK. The request includes only the app’s version, though like any download, GitHub sees your IP address.

This website has no cookies, analytics or third-party requests either.

Install

A few minutes of setup. Then you never need to open the app again.

  1. Download the APK

    Get %CHRONOTIF_APK_NAME% from its GitHub release (a .sha256 file is alongside if you want to verify it) and open it. If asked, allow your browser or file manager to install unknown apps.

    Chronotif isn’t on the Play Store, so Google Play Protect may step in. That’s a check for apps it hasn’t seen before, not a sign the APK is broken. What you’ll see varies by phone:

    1. Send app for a security check? or App scan recommended: tap Scan app (or Send) and the install continues once it’s checked.
    2. Blocked by Play Protect or Unsafe app blocked: tap More details → Install anyway.
    3. Just App not installed: pause Play Protect’s scanning for a day, then open the APK again. Installing over USB with adb install also works.
    1. Open the Play Store, tap your profile icon, then Play Protect.
    2. Tap ⚙ (top right).
    3. Switch off Scan apps with Play Protect.
    4. Choose Pause, not Turn off. Scanning turns itself back on the next day, so you don’t have to remember to.

  2. Grant notification access

    Lets Chronotif read, open, dismiss and act on your notifications. On Android 13 and newer, the first try is blocked for apps installed outside the Play Store: you’ll see App was denied access or a greyed-out toggle. That’s expected.

    1. Press Close, then open Settings → Apps → Chronotif.
    2. Tap ⋮ (top right) → Allow restricted settings, and confirm. On HyperOS it’s a switch instead: Advanced settings → Allow restricted settings, further down the same page.
    3. Return to Chronotif and tap Grant again. The toggle now works.

  3. Allow display over other apps

    Needed for the edge handle and the lock-screen view.

  4. Add the tile optional

    Add the Chronotif tile to Quick Settings. On Android 13 and newer, setup has a button that does it for you.

  5. Turn on updates optional

    In Updates, switch on Update automatically. If you also allow Install unknown apps for Chronotif (Android 12+), updates install without asking; otherwise a notification asks you to confirm each one.

Xiaomi, HyperOS & MIUI

Xiaomi adds its own permissions, and setup links straight to them. Turn on Show on Lock screen and Display pop-up windows while running in the background, enable Autostart, and set Battery saver to No restrictions.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Which phones and Android versions does it work on?

Android 11 or newer. The setup notes and screenshots come from Xiaomi phones (Poco X3 NFC and Xiaomi 17T Pro), and setup includes shortcuts for Xiaomi’s extra permissions. Other devices are less tested so far. If something doesn’t work on yours, open an issue with your device and Android version.

Is it on Google Play?

Not currently. Chronotif is distributed as an APK on GitHub Releases, or you can build it from source.

How do updates work?

Turn on Update automatically and Chronotif checks GitHub Releases once a day. When there’s a newer version, it downloads the APK, verifies it against the release’s .sha256 file and installs it. Check for updates does the same on demand, and you can always install a newer APK from Releases by hand. Android only accepts an update signed with the same key as the installed app.

Coming from v0.1.0? It has no updater and used a temporary signing key, so uninstall it once and install the new version.

Does it replace the system notification shade?

No. Android doesn’t let a third-party app replace the system shade, so Chronotif works alongside it. Swiping down from the top still opens the system shade, and heads-up banners are still the system’s. Open Chronotif with the edge handle or the Quick Settings tile instead.

How does it work with the lock screen?

The lock-screen view is shown on top of the system lock screen, which stays locked underneath. You may briefly see the system lock screen as the screen turns on. Tap outside a notification or swipe up, then use your fingerprint, face or PIN to unlock; press back to reveal the system lock screen. Left untouched for 10 seconds, it steps aside so the screen turns off as quickly as it normally would. It follows Android’s lock-screen privacy settings, never covers a ringing call or an alarm, and doesn’t appear at all if Android’s show notifications on lock screen is off. You can also turn it off in Chronotif.

Why does Android say “App was denied access”?

From Android 13, notification access is a restricted setting for apps installed outside the Play Store. Allow it once from App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings (on HyperOS, the Allow restricted settings switch under Advanced settings in App info). It only appears after you’ve tried to enable the permission. See Install for the steps.

Why is there a “Chronotif is displaying over other apps” notification?

Stock Android posts it for any app that draws over other apps, which the edge handle does. Long-press the notification to turn it off.

Does every notification look exactly like it does in the system shade?

Mostly. Big text, inbox lines, conversations, pictures, progress and actions are supported. Notifications with fully custom layouts are rendered from their text, not pixel-for-pixel.

An app complains about an overlay. What can I do?

A few apps, mostly banking apps, refuse input while any overlay is on screen. Turn off the edge handle in Chronotif and use the Quick Settings tile instead.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no in-app purchases and no paid features. Chronotif is free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0 or later: you can use, study, modify and share it. You can support development on Ko-fi. Donations are optional and don’t unlock any features.
