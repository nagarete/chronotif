# Notification wake and conversation behavior

Agreed behavior from the October 2026 bug-report interview. Implemented with automated regression coverage; verification on the reporting HyperOS device is pending.

## Wake session

An untouched notification wake is one continuous session that lasts as long as the stock lock screen would have. Chronotif remains visible until the display sleeps, without an additional interval showing the stock lock screen. Back explicitly reveals the stock lock screen.

The first reported failure was approximately ten seconds of Chronotif followed by approximately ten seconds of the stock lock screen, caused by an inactivity timer that finished the activity. Removing that timer exposed a second failure: a window covering the keyguard gets the normal screen timeout (the one used while an app is open), so the display stayed on for the full screen-timeout setting. Chronotif's window now requests the keyguard's own short timeout (`config_lockScreenDisplayTimeout`, ten seconds when the ROM doesn't define it). Touches extend it as they do on the stock lock screen. The device's display settings are not changed.

## The lock-screen view at rest

The lock-screen view wakes with the clock big and New resting at the bottom as a Pile (ADR 0011): the newest card or Conversation stack whole, one or two slivers behind it, and "N more" under it when New holds more than one. Earlier isn't shown. With nothing New, it rests as the clock and an "N earlier" line. A Media player or ongoing call sits whole under the clock, above the Pile.

With a Media player whose track has artwork, and no call going, the artwork rests large under the clock, the player under it, and the Pile under the player (ADR 0012). The artwork takes the room the Pile leaves, up to the screen's width; when too little is left, the player shows as usual. Behind everything, a blurred copy of the artwork replaces the wallpaper for as long as the player shows, also once the list is open. Scrolling up shrinks the artwork into the player's thumbnail as the list moves, while the Pile unfolds as usual. Back, or the next wake, brings it back. Only the newest player gets this; others sit under it as usual. When content is hidden on the lock screen, no artwork shows.

The Pile is the list itself, scrolled to its top. Scrolling up anywhere unfolds it under the finger: the cards folded under the top one slide out from behind it, New then Earlier, the same cards in the same order, and the slivers and count fade. The clock stays where it is until the list reaches it, then the list pushes it up and it fades, so the list takes its place. Tapping the Pile or its count unfolds it and scrolls New to the top. The Pile isn't a grouping: it holds all of New in time order and never gathers an app's cards. While it rests, only its top card can be swiped away or held for its Preview. Back folds the list back into the Pile, and Back again shows the stock lock screen. Each wake starts at the Pile.

Unlocking after the view was up for a moment checks all of New, the cards folded under the Pile included. Tapping empty space, or swiping up from *Tap to unlock* at the bottom, unlocks; swiping up anywhere else scrolls. Tapping the clock asks to unlock, then opens the alarms, and tapping the date opens the calendar at today the same way, as in the Panel's header. Pulling down at the top opens Search.

On a phone with its fingerprint sensor under the screen, *Tap to unlock* moves up until its fingerprint icon sits on the sensor, and the Pile moves up with it, so a finger on the sensor touches no card. Android has no public way to ask where the sensor is, and HyperOS keeps Xiaomi's description of it from apps, so Chronotif reads it where a phone shares it (AOSP's `config_udfps_sensor_props`, Xiaomi's `persist.vendor.sys.fp.fod.*` properties). Otherwise, when the phone shows it has such a sensor (HyperOS's fingerprint animation setting), it assumes the sensor's top is 86% of the way down the screen. *Settings → Lock screen → Fit to your fingerprint sensor*, offered only on those phones, takes one touch on the sensor and fits the room to it exactly. Phones with the sensor on the side or the back keep the usual layout.

## Double-tap to sleep

Double-tapping empty space on the lock-screen view puts the display to sleep, as double-tapping the stock lock screen does on Xiaomi, Samsung and OnePlus phones (ADR 0014). Empty space is where a tap means nothing but "unlock": not the clock or the date, a card, the Pile, a Media player or its artwork, a section header, *Tap to unlock* or a Lock-screen shortcut. The blank gap between the clock and the Pile is empty space, and so is the room beside the cards.

Android has no call that lets an app turn the display off. So the screen turns black at once, at the lowest brightness and without the status and navigation bars, and Android puts the display to sleep when its shortest screen timeout runs out: 6 seconds after the double tap on HyperOS. Until then the black screen acts as a sleeping one: a double tap brings the view back, as double tap to wake would, and a single tap, Back or a swipe does nothing. A power press in that time finishes the sleep, so a second press wakes the phone. Going to sleep this way isn't a check, just as the display timing out isn't.

To tell one tap from two, a single tap on empty space waits Android's double-tap timeout (about 0.3 seconds) before asking to unlock. A tap on a header asks at once, and a tap on the clock or the date asks to unlock and then opens the alarms or the calendar. *Settings → Lock screen → Double-tap to sleep*, on by default, turns the gesture off, and a tap on empty space then asks to unlock without the wait. It works only on the lock-screen view, not on the Panel or on Search opened over the lock screen.

## Lock-screen shortcuts

The lock-screen view covers the stock lock screen, and with it the stock lock screen's corner shortcuts, so it has two of its own: one on each side of *Tap to unlock*, in the same row. They show for the whole wake, at rest, with the list unfolded and while it scrolls, and rise with the row over an in-screen fingerprint sensor. They hide under a Preview, the image viewer, Search, and the black screen of Double-tap to sleep.

Pressing and holding one uses it. While held, the button brightens and grows until the hold counts, then settles back with a haptic click. On HyperOS the buttons are a faint dark circle; elsewhere they're darker with a light rim and brighten more when held. The faint circle nearly vanishes on a dark wallpaper, so this is a stopgap until the buttons get a frosted look that reads on any wallpaper. A tap only shows *Hold to use*: a tap elsewhere on the view unlocks, and a pocket shouldn't turn the flashlight on. The click follows the phone's haptic feedback setting: with it off, Android drops every app's touch vibration, so only the button shows the press. *Settings → Lock screen → Shortcuts* and the Tutorial's step then say so, with a way to Android's Sound & vibration settings. Each corner is set under *Settings → Lock screen → Shortcuts* to one of:

- **Flashlight**: on or off, without unlocking. Its button lights while the torch is on, whichever way it was turned on, and the torch stays on after unlocking, as with the system tile. While another app films, it can't turn on and says so.
- **Camera**: the phone's camera in its secure mode, over the lock screen, without unlocking. Video is picked inside the camera.
- **QR scanner**: Google Lens, after an unlock (ADR 0017). Without Lens it says *Not available without Google Lens* and offers *Get Google Lens*, which opens the Play Store after an unlock.
- **Do Not Disturb**: on or off with no end time, as the system tile does; the button lights while it's on. It only silences: every card still shows, and New is the same.
- **An app**: picked from the apps Search finds, shown by its icon; holding it asks to unlock, then opens it.
- **None**: the corner stays empty.

Flashlight on the left and Camera on the right is the default, for installs from before the shortcuts too, since the view had been covering the stock ones. One choice can't be in both corners, except None and An app. In Settings, without Google Lens, the QR scanner can't be picked: its row says it isn't available and offers *Get Google Lens*.

The **Chronotif QR scanner** tile in Quick Settings opens Lens the same way, after Android's unlock when locked. Without Lens it still shows, with *Needs Google Lens* under its name, and tapping it gives the same message and button. *Settings → Lock screen → Shortcuts* offers to add the tile, on Android 13 and later, while Lens is installed and the tile isn't added. Its name carries "Chronotif" so it isn't taken for the phone's own scanner tile, as with Chronotif Search.

## Conversation stacks

Revised in the October 2026 interview comparing Chronotif with iOS (ADR 0002). Each conversation that lists its messages is shown as a Conversation stack: one card per incoming message, under the conversation's title and Avatar, with the message's own time.

Its messages are split at the last check. Those sent since then stack in New, and the ones already checked stack in Earlier, so one conversation can appear in both. At least the latest message is New when the conversation is, because message times come from the sender's clock. There is no limit on how many messages a stack holds, beyond what Android currently supplies.

A stack with one message is a plain card. A stack with more is collapsed: the latest message, a message count on the Avatar, and slivers underneath. Tapping it opens one card per message, newest first, under a header with the conversation's title, Show less and ×. Tapping the title collapses it again, the same as Show less.

- Tapping a message card opens the chat.
- Its chevron shows the app's buttons; Reply and Mark as read act on the whole conversation.
- Swiping one message card hides only that message in Chronotif, because Android can't remove one message from a notification. The chat stays in the system shade.
- Swiping a collapsed stack, or tapping ×, clears the whole conversation, which removes its stacks from both sections.

In Earlier, each such conversation has its own stack. Your own replies aren't cards. Apps that send only the latest text, with no list of messages, can't be split: they stay one card per notification. When content is hidden on the lock screen, a conversation is one card with no messages.

This covers messages available in active Android notifications. It does not require an archive of messages Android no longer supplies.

## App stacks in Earlier

Agreed in the November 2026 review of Earlier on the phone (ADR 0004, which supersedes ADR 0003).

Earlier is newest first. Cards from one app that sit next to each other in that order fold into an App stack, as long as each arrived within 30 minutes of the stack's newest card. Three T3 Code cards from the last two minutes form one stack. A T3 Code card from 4 hours earlier forms a separate stack with the cards near it, never with the recent ones. A card from another app between two of an app's cards splits them. Grouping all of an app's cards together would pull its older cards above newer ones from other apps.

A collapsed App stack shows its newest card with slivers underneath. Tapping it opens its cards in the same place, under the app's name with Show less and ×. Tapping the name collapses it again. Swiping the collapsed stack, or tapping ×, clears every notification in it. A conversation showing one message is a single card and can join an App stack, for example TikTok's one-message chats. A Conversation stack with several messages stays on its own. New doesn't use App stacks: every New card shows in full. The "N earlier" pill counts cards, not stacks.

## New incoming messages

A new incoming message returns its conversation to New, including when the app sends the update silently or marks it to alert only once. Timestamp refreshes, progress updates, and updates with no new incoming message do not return a checked conversation to New merely because they were reposted.

For structured conversations, incoming message timestamps identify arrivals. A conversation Chronotif sees for the first time (after a restart, or when an app re-alerts old chats) arrived with its latest message, not when it was posted, so yesterday's chat stays in Earlier. Conversations sort by their newest message, the time their cards show. Outgoing replies and changes to historic messages do not count. For apps that supply only a message title and body, changed text identifies an arrival; Android supplies no incoming-message identity in that format. Identical plain-text messages cannot be distinguished from reposts.

## Avatars and Attachments

Agreed in the October 2026 interview comparing Chronotif with the stock shade and iOS.

A conversation card leads with its Avatar, badged with the app's icon. A group shows its picture, falling back to the latest sender's photo. A one-to-one chat shows the sender's photo, falling back to the notification's large icon. With no picture at all, the card keeps the plain app icon. Other notifications keep their large icon on the right. An app that draws its own layout and gives no large icon (TikTok's LIVE alerts carry the host's photo only there) gets the biggest photo it put into that layout instead. Images built into the layout itself, such as the app's logo, don't count.

An Attachment shows as a thumbnail on the right of its message's card, as on iOS, on New, Earlier and open stacks alike. A video's thumbnail carries a play mark. A collapsed Conversation stack shows its latest message's thumbnail, with "+n" for the other photos inside. Tapping the thumbnail opens the app, like the rest of the card.

Pressing and holding any card opens its Preview over a dimmed screen, without unlocking. It shows the card's full text, its Attachment (or a picture notification's picture) large, and the app's buttons. Reply types inside the Preview, on the lock-screen view too, without unlocking (ADR 0013). Only when the app marks its reply as needing an unlock does the lock-screen view ask for one and continue in the panel. The app's other buttons run without unlocking when they work in the background, such as Mark as read; a button that opens the app, or one the app marks as needing an unlock, asks to unlock first. Tapping outside, or Back, closes it. Nothing in the app is opened or marked read.

Under the app's buttons the Preview has Snooze and Clear. Clear does what swiping the card would: a message card hides only that message, a collapsed stack clears everything in it, and any other card clears its notification. Snooze appears for clearable notifications, not for calls, Media players or Running. It opens into 15 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours, Tomorrow morning (8:00 the next day, or the same day before 4:00) and Pick a time…. Pick a time shows a time picker, starting an hour from now, and a button that says when the notification comes back. A time already past today means tomorrow. Snoozing a message snoozes its whole conversation, because Android snoozes notifications, not messages. Android hides the notification, from the system shade too, and posts it again when the time is up. It comes back as New, at the time it came back, even if nothing in it changed (ADR 0010). Neither needs unlocking, as swiping doesn't.

*Settings → Panel → Swipe to clear* turns off clearing by swiping, in the Panel and on the lock-screen view. Cards then give a little and spring back when swiped sideways, without opening anything, and clear from their Preview or a section's Clear. Paused Media players can still be swiped away, since they have no Preview.

Tapping the Preview's image opens a full-screen viewer, still without unlocking. In the viewer you pinch or double-tap to zoom, swipe sideways through the conversation's images, and swipe down or press Back to close. Save puts the image in the gallery's Pictures/Chronotif album. Share asks to unlock first on the lock screen, because it opens another app. A video shows only its still frame, so it offers Open in app instead of Save and Share. A notification's expanded picture opens in the same viewer. Viewing doesn't count as a check. When content is hidden, there are no Avatars or Attachments.

Chronotif reads Attachments through the sending app's content links, which Android lets notification listeners read. Many apps send a shrunk preview rather than the original, so a saved image can be lower quality than the one in the chat. An Attachment that can't be read just isn't shown.

## Calls, media and Running

Agreed in the October 2026 interview about always-running services crowding the top of the panel.

Ongoing calls come first, then Media players, then New and Earlier. Every other ongoing notification is Running: always-running services, connected devices, step counters, downloads, navigation and timers. Running sits below Earlier behind an "N running" pill and collapses again each time the panel opens. When Running is all there is, only the pill shows.

A media player stays a Media player while paused, even though apps drop the ongoing flag when they pause. It never falls into Earlier. Calls, Media players and Running never count as New. Calls and Running never bring up the lock-screen view by themselves. A playing Media player does, unless the lock-screen view is off, so waking the phone during playback shows its artwork and controls; a paused one doesn't, since apps leave paused players around for hours (ADR 0012). On the lock-screen view, Running sits below Earlier behind its pill, as in the Panel.

A Media player is drawn from the app's media session, like the system shade's player: artwork, title and artist, previous / play-pause / next, and a seek bar you can drag with elapsed and total time. It has no output (cast) button, because Android offers apps no public way to open the output picker. Apps that share no session fall back to their notification actions. When several apps have players, all of them show, newest first. Paused or stopped Media players can be swiped left or right when the app allows it. A playing player cannot be swiped away. If Android keeps the notification after a swipe, the card returns. On the lock-screen view, players follow the same privacy rules as other cards: when content is hidden, only the app name and controls show.

## Opening the Panel

The status-bar swipe brings the Panel down under the finger, like the system shade: it appears with the first downward movement and follows it. Let go past a third of the screen, or flick down, and it opens; otherwise it slides back up. A tap on the status bar opens it at once, and so do the edge handle and the Quick Settings tile. While the Panel follows the finger, the light dim behind it follows too; the blur comes in once it has opened.

The header and dim show at once and the cards right after. A long list fills in from the top. Every open starts like a new one: cards collapsed, no reply box, scrolled to the top.

Being on screen counts as a check once the Panel's cards have shown for about 1.5 seconds and it has opened fully, or as soon as it's used. For a pull that opens, the 1.5 seconds count from when its cards first showed during the pull. A pull that slides back is never a check.

After an update, the first Panel open shows What's new on a sheet over the cards: the release notes this version has that weren't shown before, so a nightly that adds a line shows just that line. It shows once, however it's closed. A new install shows nothing, and the first update from a version without What's new shows only the new version's own notes. While the sheet covers the cards the Panel isn't seen: closing it then is no check, and the 1.5 seconds count from when the sheet goes. The lock-screen view never shows it.

## Search

Search opens without the accessibility service, so it works alongside banking apps that refuse to run with one (ADR 0008). It opens from five places: a pull-down in the Panel or lock-screen view, a long press on the edge handle, the Chronotif Search Quick Settings tile, a Search home-screen widget (a bar 2 to 5 cells wide, or a round 1x1 button), and the Search shortcut on a long press of the app icon. Launchers that bind gestures, such as Nova, can bind theirs to the shortcut. Opened anywhere but the pull-down, Search has its own window over whatever is on screen, the lock screen included. It never takes the digital-assistant slot, so Gemini keeps the power button.

Pulling down with the list at its top, on the Panel or the lock-screen view, opens Search. On the Panel the pull has to start after the Panel has finished opening. The status-bar swipe that opens it ends when the finger lifts, so it never runs on into Search. A pull shorter than about 80dp springs back. The field gets focus and the keyboard comes up.

Search never searches notifications. Before you type, it shows a row of up to four apps, the ones that notified most recently, newest first, once each. That counts an app whose notification has since been dismissed. Calls, Media players, Running and Chronotif itself don't count. Typing hides the row and shows, top to bottom: an answer, apps, contacts, Settings pages, calendar events, and a web row.

The answer is one card for quick math (`12*7.5+3`, `200 + 15%`), a unit conversion (`5 km in mi`, `30 c` goes to °F), the time in a city or one-time-zone country (`time in tokyo`, `bali time`), or a currency (`100 usd`, `$20`, `rp 50.000 to sgd`). Numbers are read and written the way the phone's language does: in English `50,000` is fifty thousand, in Indonesian `50.000`. Math shows up to 10 significant digits and conversions 6, with trailing zeros dropped. Tapping the card copies the number without grouping, also on the lock screen, since a result reveals nothing.

Currency reads a code, symbol or name before or after the amount (`100 usd`, `$100`, `€20`, `rp 50000`), and an optional target (`100 usd to sgd`). `$` is US dollars and `¥` yen; "pound" stays a unit of mass, so pounds sterling are `£` or `gbp`. Without a target, an amount converts to the currency of the country the phone is in, by its mobile network, then its SIM, then its language's region; an amount already in that currency converts to US dollars. Rupiah, yen, won, forint and krónur show whole amounts, and `rp 50.000` is fifty thousand in any language, since those have no cents. Other currencies show two decimals. The rates are the European Central Bank's daily reference rates, which cover about 30 currencies, and the card shows their date. They download only when *Settings → Search → Currency rates* is on and a currency question meets saved rates older than the newest the ECB should have out. The switch is on for new installs; installs from before 0.2.7 keep it off until it's turned on (ADR 0015). It publishes on working days around 16:00 Frankfurt time, so the app looks for a new day's rates after 16:15 there, and on a bank holiday, when the same rates come back, it looks again at most once an hour. Nothing typed is sent. With the switch off, the card offers to turn it on.

Apps are listed by name: those whose name starts with the query, then those where a later word starts with it ("music" finds YouTube Music), then those containing it anywhere ("tok" finds TikTok), alphabetical within each. Case and accents don't matter. A work-profile or dual copy of an app is its own entry, with the profile's badge.

Contacts and calendar events each need a switch in *Settings → Search*, off until turned on. Turning one on asks for Android's Contacts or Calendar permission, and the switch stays off if it's refused. Both are read on the phone when Search opens and never stored. A contact matches when its name, or a later word in it, starts with the query, so "bu" finds Budi but not Ibu. Typed digits, three or more, also find a contact by number. The best three show, then *Show more* lists up to twenty. Each row has the photo or initial, the name, and the number its buttons use: the one set as default, else a mobile, else the first. Tapping the row opens the contact. For a contact on WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business, the row says so and the message button opens their WhatsApp chat, using WhatsApp when a contact is on both. Otherwise it opens the default SMS app to that number. The call button opens the dialer with the number ready, unless *Settings → Search → Call from Search* is on. That switch asks for Android's Phone permission, because WhatsApp starts a call only for an app holding it. With it on, the call button starts a WhatsApp voice call for a WhatsApp contact and a phone call for anyone else, and WhatsApp contacts get a video call button too. Calendar events match their title the same way, from 30 days ago to a year ahead, and the three soonest show: events not over yet by start time, then past ones, the latest first. A repeating event shows once, at its next time, and so does the same event in two calendars, such as a holiday in two holiday calendars. Tapping one opens it in the calendar app.

*Settings → Search* opens with a checklist like General's: a card with a ring, how many steps are left and the next one, then a row per step. The steps are *Find contacts*, *Call from Search*, *Currency rates*, *Find calendar events* and *Add the Search widget*. Each row's button does the step: Allow asks for the permission, Turn on switches currency rates on, and Add asks the home screen to place the Search bar. A home screen that doesn't let apps place widgets gets directions instead. Each step also has *Not now*, which stops it counting as left, so the card can say *All set* without every permission; the row keeps its button, and the switch below still turns it on. A step counts as done only while its switch is on and Android still grants its permission, and the widget step while a Search widget sits on a home screen. *Add the Search tile* is listed last as optional and never counts: on Android 13 and newer its button asks Android to add the tile, and on older versions the row says how to add it by hand. The drawer shows the steps left beside Search, as it does for General. The switches below follow what the checklist turns on.

Settings pages come from a fixed list of the pages Android lets apps open, matched by name and common words ("wifi", "brightness", "hotspot"), at most four. The web row is always last: text that looks like an address (`klikbca.com`) opens directly in the default browser, anything else is searched there with the engine chosen in *Settings → Search* (Google by default). Go on the keyboard opens the top app, or the web when no app matches. Chronotif sends nothing itself.

Results show while locked, contacts and calendar events included (ADR 0013). Only when the lock screen hides sensitive content (*Settings → Lock screen → Hide sensitive content*, or the system's setting with a secure lock) are contacts and events held back: with either switch on, a row saying *Unlock to see all results* takes their place, whether or not anything matched, so the lock screen doesn't reveal who or what is in them. Tapping it asks to unlock, then shows everything for the same query. Tapping an app, a Settings page or the web row on the lock screen asks to unlock, then opens it. Search shows no notifications, so opening it is never a check. Back, a tap on empty space, or a swipe up closes Search. On the lock-screen view, closing goes back to the notifications rather than unlocking. *Settings → Search → Pull down for Search* turns the pull-down off; the other ways in stay.

## Tutorial

*Settings → Tutorial*, above Tips, runs Chronotif hands-on with made-up notifications. Each part is a few steps; a step says what to try, shows *✓ That's it* once the user did, and Back and Continue move between steps whether or not they tried. The first part, Notifications, has six:

1. Open the Panel with the edge handle (or the status-bar swipe when it's on). The tutorial screen also offers to add the Chronotif tile to Quick Settings on Android 13 and newer, and says how to add it by hand on older versions.
2. New and Earlier. The Panel opens with three samples in New (Daybook, Parcelly, and Maya Chen's two Murmur messages as a Conversation stack) and two in Earlier (Fernbank, Stride). Maya writes again a moment later. Closing the Panel after it was seen, then opening it again, moves what was seen to Earlier.
3. Open the stack and close it by its name.
4. Hold a card for its Preview, and reply or snooze from there.
5. Swipe a card to clear it.
6. Close the Panel.

While a part runs, the Panel, however it's opened, shows only the samples, with the step's card at its bottom; cards scroll up clear of it, and it steps aside for the keyboard. The samples have a last check of their own, so New and Earlier work as usual without touching the real one. Opening, replying, clearing, hiding a message and snoozing change only the samples: nothing reaches Android or an app, and a sample has nothing to open, so tapping it opens it in place. What's new waits for the real Panel. The edge handle's tint, the header and Search stay real.

The second part, Search, has six steps over the same samples: pull down in the Panel for Search; hold the edge handle for Search over anything; ask Search something (`12*7.5+3`, `5 km in mi`, `time in tokyo`); find an app and tap it; add the Search widget (and, as an extra, the Search tile; holding Chronotif's icon and binding a launcher's swipe to its Search shortcut are described); and see what Search can do once allowed, with each step of Search's checklist and whether it's done, and a button to that checklist in Settings, where doing it counts in both places. While the Search part runs, Search says what a tap would open (an app, a Settings page, the browser, a contact's app, the calendar) instead of opening it. The widget and checklist steps count once the user is back on the tutorial screen with them done. Finishing a part offers the next, keeping the samples, so it can go on in the Panel that's open.

The third part, Lock screen, runs on a stand-in of the lock-screen view, since the real one only shows while the phone is locked. *Show the lock screen* brings it up with the samples resting as the Pile, and the step's card between the list and *Tap to unlock*. Eight steps: drag the list up to unfold the Pile and press Back to fold it; tap the Pile; hold Maya's card and reply from its Preview; tap the clock or the date; pull down for Search; double-tap empty space; hold a Lock-screen shortcut; and pick when Chronotif shows on the lock screen (Off, *When something is new*, *Whenever there are notifications*), which sets *Settings → Lock screen → Show on lock screen*. The stand-in stays up while the phone is unlocked and never asks Android to unlock: what would wait for an unlock (the clock, the date, an app from Search, tapping empty space) says so in a message instead. Holding a Lock-screen shortcut says what it would do, and does nothing. A double tap turns it black for a moment and back, with a message that the real display would sleep now; the screen timeout isn't touched. Samples always show in full there, since nothing in them is private, and the stand-in records no lock-screen check.

A run lives only in memory. Leaving the tutorial screen, the Panel, the lock-screen stand-in and Search (opened from the edge handle) ends it, as does Android stopping Chronotif, and the next Panel shows the real notifications. The step reached is saved, so Settings shows *Left at step n* and Continue picks up there. A finished part shows Done and can be started again.

## Verification cases

- Leave a notification wake untouched. Chronotif remains visible and the display sleeps after the stock lock-screen timeout (about ten seconds), not the normal screen timeout, without an extra stock lock-screen interval.
- Wake the phone with three New notifications. The clock is big, the newest card sits whole at the bottom with slivers behind it and "2 more"; Earlier isn't shown. Swipe the top card sideways: it clears and the next one takes its place.
- Drag the list up slowly from the Pile. The cards slide out from under the top one as the finger moves, with no jump; the clock stays until the first card reaches it, then is pushed up and fades. Tap the Pile instead: it unfolds and scrolls New to the top.
- Scroll the list open and press Back: it folds back into the Pile. Press Back again: the stock lock screen appears. Swipe up from *Tap to unlock*: the unlock prompt shows. Fling the list up: it only scrolls.
- Wake the phone with something New and double-tap the gap between the clock and the Pile. The screen goes black at once, and the display sleeps about 6 seconds later without the stock lock screen showing. ## Automated verification

`./gradlew testDebugUnitTest` runs the pure attention-model tests and Android-backed Robolectric tests. Regression tests reproduce the silent message being classified into Earlier and the ten-second activity exit before the fixes. They cover the split of a conversation at the last check, collapsed and opened Conversation stacks, and hiding a single message. They also cover outgoing replies, historic messages, repeated content, plain-text message updates, and redaction.

The lock-activity test verifies that the window requests the keyguard timeout, that inactivity leaves Chronotif visible, and that a screen-off broadcast closes it. The double-tap tests verify that a double tap on empty space turns the screen black, asks for the shortest timeout and never asks to unlock. They also check that only a double tap brings the view back, that a single tap waits before asking to unlock, that the clock isn't empty space, and that turning the switch off removes the wait. On an Android 16 emulator with a 60-second screen timeout, an untouched wake slept after about 11 seconds with Chronotif visible; without the timeout request it stayed on for 60 seconds. HyperOS power management is not emulated. Verify that behavior on the reporting phone.
